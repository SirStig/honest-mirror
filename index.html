Why this exists

The word is everywhere. The answers aren't.

“Narcissist,” “manipulator,” “controlling,” “selfish.” These words get thrown around in arguments, breakups, group chats, and comment sections every day. Sometimes they're true. Sometimes they're a way of saying “you hurt me.” Sometimes they're used to hurt you.

If you keep hearing them, it's confusing and it hurts. And the quizzes online make it worse: a handful of obvious questions like “Do you like being the center of attention?” that anyone can answer however they want.

What we believe

  • Labels shouldn't be thrown at people casually. Including at yourself.
  • Everyone has some of these traits. It's about how much, and how often.
  • Only a real professional can diagnose. A good tool can still help you see clearly.
  • Honest self-reflection takes courage, and it deserves to be taken seriously.

How it's different

Built to make honest answers easier than flattering ones

Instead of asking “Are you a narcissist?” in a dozen ways, Honest Mirror comes at the question from nine different angles, several of them borrowed from how researchers reduce faking.

Real-life moments

Everyday situations where every option is something reasonable people do. No obvious “good” answer to hide behind.

This-or-that choices

Two equally acceptable options. Forced choices like these are one of the best-studied ways to reduce faking.

What you actually did

Concrete questions about the last 30 days. Recent behavior is much harder to bend than opinions about yourself.

Reading people

Short illustrated stories that measure empathy as a skill, so you can't just claim it.

Hidden honesty checks

Repeated questions, too-good-to-be-true statements, and a few made-up terms show how much your results can be trusted.

Quick reactions

Words flash for three seconds. Gut reactions leave less room to think about how an answer looks.

People who know you

Send a private link to a friend, partner, or family member and compare how they see you with how you see yourself.

Read aloud

Every question can be read to you, so you can take the test comfortably however you prefer.

Questions

Common questions

Can an online test tell me if I'm a narcissist?

No, and you should be wary of any site that says it can. Only a licensed mental health professional can diagnose Narcissistic Personality Disorder, usually through detailed interviews. What this test can do is show which traits came through in your answers, how strongly, and how much those answers can be trusted. That gives you something real to reflect on or bring to a therapist.

Isn't it easy to lie on these tests?

On most short quizzes, yes. We can't make lying impossible, but we make it harder and we can tell when it's probably happening. We use scenarios with no obvious right answer, forced choices, concrete behavior questions, a skill-based empathy section, consistency checks, too-good-to-be-true statements, made-up terms, timed reactions, and optional ratings from people who know you. See exactly how it works.

Are my answers stored or shared?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Your progress is saved only on your own device so you can pause and come back. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no server receiving your answers. Clearing your browser data erases it.

If I'm worried I'm a narcissist, does that mean I'm not?

Not necessarily. It's a popular myth. People with narcissistic traits can be self-aware, especially those with more vulnerable, insecure traits. Worrying about how you affect others is a very good sign and a great place to start, but it isn't proof either way.

Someone keeps calling me a narcissist. Are they right?

Maybe, maybe partly, or maybe not at all. Sometimes the word points at a real pattern. Sometimes it's shorthand for “I feel hurt and unheard.” And sometimes it's used as a weapon. Our guide walks through how to tell the difference.

Can I use this to find out if my partner or parent is a narcissist?

This test is designed for looking at yourself. Answering on someone else's behalf won't give meaningful results. If you're trying to make sense of someone in your life, start with our guide. If you feel unsafe, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free and confidential.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no paywall, no email required. It's an open project hosted for free on GitHub Pages.
