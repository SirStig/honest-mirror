The problem with short quizzes

Ask someone “Do you think you're special?” and they know what the question is fishing for. Psychologists call this socially desirable responding: answering in a way that looks good, to others and to ourselves. It's usually not deliberate lying.

No self-report test can eliminate this. Several well-studied techniques can reduce it and detect it, and this test combines them.

The nine parts

  1. 1. How you see yourself

    Subtle statements, some worded in reverse.

  2. 2. Moments

    Realistic situations where every response is something reasonable people do. Situational judgment tests like these are widely used in hiring because they're harder to game than direct questions.

  3. 3. This or that

    A choice between two acceptable options. Research suggests forced choices can reduce faking compared with agree/disagree scales.

  4. 4. Reading people

    Short stories that ask what someone is most likely feeling, which checks a skill instead of a claim. It's inspired by research on “theory of mind” tasks, though much simpler. Missing these can reflect neurodivergence, fatigue, or cultural differences, so results explain them without blame.

  5. 5. The last 30 days

    “Did you give someone the silent treatment this month?” is harder to spin than “Are you manipulative?” Recent behavior is a stronger signal than general opinions about ourselves.

  6. 6. You and other people

    How you rank yourself against others, and how you think others behave. Most people rate themselves above average, and cynical views of others have been linked to manipulative tendencies.

  7. 7. A few more about you

    More statements, which help show how consistent your answers were.

  8. 8. General knowledge

    A short familiarity check. Your results page explains why it's there, because explaining it first would spoil it.

  9. 9. Quick reactions

    Words flash for about three seconds each, leaving less room to think about how an answer looks. It's a noisy measure, so it's weighted modestly.

Every question can also be read aloud.

People who know you

Research on self-knowledge suggests other people often see traits like arrogance or defensiveness more clearly than we do. After the test you can send a private link to someone who knows you. They answer 16 statements about you and send back a short code, which you paste into your results.

Scoring

Every answer feeds one or more of eight traits: grandiosity, entitlement, fragile self-worth, empathy gap, defensiveness, manipulative tactics, control, and self-focus. Behavior questions and scenarios count a little more than general statements. Each trait gets a score from 0 to 100, grouped as Faint, Some, Noticeable, or Strong.

Separately, built-in honesty checks produce an answer quality rating. We don't list them here, because knowing them in advance would weaken them. We don't secretly adjust your scores: your results show each check and what it means.

Everything is calculated in your browser using code you can read yourself.

Limits

  • It's not validated. The questions are original and informed by research, but the test hasn't been studied in a clinical trial or compared against professional diagnoses. Scores are not percentiles.
  • It can't diagnose. Narcissistic Personality Disorder and other conditions can only be diagnosed by a qualified professional.
  • Context matters. Depression, anxiety, trauma, burnout, ADHD, and autism can all affect answers in ways that might look like these traits but aren't.
  • A determined person can still fake it. We make it harder and more visible, not impossible.
  • No audio-based scoring. There's no reliable scientific way to detect narcissism from someone's voice or their reaction to sounds or images, so we don't pretend to.

Privacy

Honest Mirror is a static website with no database, no account, no analytics, no cookies, and no server that receives your answers. Answers are saved in your browser's local storage so you can pause and come back. Clearing your browser data, or pressing “Retake the test,” removes them.

Invite links contain only the first name you type in. The code a friend sends back contains only their answers as numbers, plus how they know you and how well. It's never uploaded anywhere.

GitHub Pages hosts the site and, like any web host, may keep standard request logs. The site loads nothing from any other third party. Its fonts are served from the same place as its pages.

Approach

  • We describe traits, not people. Results never say “you are a narcissist.”
  • Every trait comes with what it isn't, and with small, practical things to try.
  • We point people to professionals and crisis resources throughout.
  • We discourage using the test to label someone else.
